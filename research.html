Finding the missing diagnoses

Positive-unlabeled learning for undiagnosed type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease in long-term primary care records

Problem

A diagnosis code in a claims record is strong evidence that a condition exists. The absence of a code is weak evidence that it does not. In primary care, conditions such as type 2 diabetes and early chronic kidney disease frequently go unrecorded, either because they are never diagnosed or because they are managed without being coded.

Most prediction models trained on claims treat "no code" as "no disease." That assumption biases both the models and any prevalence estimate built on them. It also hides a second problem: how completely diagnoses are recorded is not fixed. It shifts with clinical practice patterns, coding rules, and changes in the clinicians who see patients over the years.

Research questions

  1. How complete is diagnostic recording for diabetes and chronic kidney disease when measured against laboratory evidence, and how does completeness vary over time?
  2. Can positive-unlabeled learning that allows the labeling mechanism to change over time identify patients who likely have these conditions but carry no recorded diagnosis?
  3. How well do models trained in one period transfer to periods with different recording behavior?

Approach

Recorded diagnoses form the labeled positive set; all other patients are unlabeled rather than negative. Laboratory results interpreted with standard clinical thresholds (for example HbA1c and fasting glucose for diabetes, eGFR and albuminuria for chronic kidney disease) provide partial ground truth for estimating recording completeness and for evaluation.

Because recording behavior is not constant, the standard assumption that labeled positives are selected completely at random is unlikely to hold. The project therefore focuses on PU learning under a time-varying labeling mechanism, with evaluation on held-out laboratory-confirmed cases and attention to calibration, not only discrimination.

Expected contributions

  • An estimate of diagnostic under-recording in long-term primary care, grounded in laboratory evidence
  • A PU learning formulation suited to labels whose completeness drifts over time
  • A case study of applying weak supervision to a single-site, multi-decade dataset

Status

Proposal stage. A pre-registered analysis plan is in preparation. Code and detailed methods will be released with the first preprint.
